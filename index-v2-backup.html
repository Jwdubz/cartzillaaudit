What your site is costing you — Cartzilla  —

I'm Jarrett, a Las Vegas web designer. I went through your site the way a group planning a night on the Strip would: find the shopping-cart ride, check the price, book. The foundation is clear: the price (from $69 per person), the one-hour length, the seven-passenger cart and a Book Now button are easy to find, you have a page for each kind of group, and a search tool named you in four of five searches. Checked October 17, 2026

What I found

01

Your homepage sends a phone about 30 MB.

In a simulated slow-connection load test on a phone-size screen, the homepage made 96 requests and transferred 30,999 KiB. The biggest pieces were a 13,258 KiB booking script from build.tripworks.com, your 8,788 KiB homepage video and a 3,805 KiB photo. The speed score was 28 and 35 in two runs, and the main content showed at 97.2 and 97.3 seconds. The Las Vegas Strip Tour page scored 27 and transferred 18,616 KiB. This is a lab simulation of a slow phone connection; a phone on fast Wi-Fi will be quicker, and field speed is unmeasured.

02

Search and social tools get an empty page.

Your homepage's HTML is 5,841 bytes: a title, a description and a script. It has no heading, no page text, no business markup and no canonical address, so anything that doesn't run scripts sees none of your wording. The share preview image points to a Replit test address (cartzilla-redesign--calebgay7.replit.app/opengraph.jpg). It loads today, but it isn't on your own domain.

03

Three different descriptions of where the ride starts.

Your homepage footer and Contact page say “Pick-up On Strip, Las Vegas, NV 89107”. Your Las Vegas Strip Tour page says “Pickup Near The Strip”. Your group pages say “Head to the meeting point at Treasure Island” and “Tours depart from Treasure Island on the north end of the Strip”. None of them gives a street address.

04

Buttons and colors that assistive tools can't read.

The accessibility score was 89 on the homepage and the Strip Tour page. The mobile menu button has no accessible name, so a screen reader announces just “button”. Five elements fail the color-contrast check, headings skip levels, and images have no width and height set, which lets the page jump while it loads.

05

Your sitemap and llms.txt send mixed signals.

Your sitemap lists 14 pages. The homepage, Strip Tour, About and Contact pages are dated December 17, 2024, while the group pages carry dates from 2026. /llms.txt answers with a web page (HTML), not a plain-text guide. It is a newer, optional file that some AI tools read, so this is a small point.

What's working

The price (from $69 per person), the one-hour length, the seven-passenger cart and a Book Now button are easy to find. You have a page for each kind of group (bachelor, bachelorette, birthday, corporate, team building and large groups). Your robots.txt allows every crawler and lists the sitemap, and every page I opened has its own title and description. The mobile menu aside, every page loaded without errors.

Unmeasured

Visitor numbers, rankings, how the booking flow behaves end to end (I didn't book anything), your Google and Tripadvisor listings as those sites show them, and the real speed on a customer's phone. I didn't call or text anyone.

What I checked on your site

  • Found
    Not blocking AI crawlers

    Your robots.txt allows every crawler, names Googlebot, Bingbot and others, and lists your sitemap. It disallows only /test.

  • Found
    Each page has a title and description

    The homepage title is “Cartzilla Ride | Las Vegas Strip Tour Guide Experience”, with a written description.

  • Gap
    Page text depends on a script

    The homepage HTML is 5,841 bytes with no page text. Tools that don't run JavaScript see only the title and description.

  • Gap
    No business markup

    The homepage carries no LocalBusiness or Product markup, so your price, hours, phone and address are not given to search tools in a machine-readable form.

  • Gap
    Share preview image is on a test site

    The og:image tag points to a Replit address instead of your own domain.

  • Note
    No canonical address on the homepage

    The homepage HTML has no canonical link tag.

  • Note
    Sitemap dates

    Four core pages are dated December 17, 2024, while the group pages carry 2026 dates.

  • Note
    llms.txt shows a web page

    /llms.txt answers with HTML rather than a plain-text guide.

What I couldn't measure

  • Rankings in Google or Google Maps.
  • What ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI answers say when asked directly. I didn't ask those assistants myself, so the searches above are a stand-in.
  • How many people search for these terms or how many visit your site.

  • How your pages look and behave once the script has run on a real phone: my speed test was a lab simulation of a slow connection, not a person using a phone.
